6.01 — Characteristics of Polynomials

The vocabulary of polynomials: terms, degree, standard form, and what's NOT a polynomial. Lock this in and the rest of Module 6 gets easy.

10 practice problems ~30 min Module 6 start

A. What is a polynomial?

A polynomial is an expression made of terms added or subtracted together. Each term is a number (the coefficient) times one or more variables raised to whole-number powers (0, 1, 2, 3, …).

Examples

3x2 − 5x + 7    x3y + 4xy − 2    9    −4x

Even just 9 counts (constant) and −4x counts (monomial).

B. Counting terms

Terms are separated by + or −. The name depends on how many there are:

TermsNameExample
1Monomial7x3
2Binomial2x + 5
3Trinomialx2 + 3x − 4
4+(just) Polynomialx3 − 2x2 + x − 6
Tip: Count by looking at the + and − signs between terms. 5x − 4 + 2y3x − 9 has 4 terms (a polynomial).

C. Degree — single variable

With one variable, the degree of a term is the variable's exponent. The degree of the polynomial is the highest degree among its terms.

Example

In 2x4 − 8x5 − 2x2 + 5:
Term degrees: 4, 5, 2, 0. Polynomial degree = 5 (the highest).

D. Degree with multiple variables ⚠️

This is the #1 trap. For a term with more than one variable, the term's degree is the SUM of the exponents, not just one of them.
Example — the trap

3y3x has exponents 3 (on y) and 1 (on x). Its degree = 3 + 1 = 4, not 3.

Worked example

What is the degree of 9x2y − 4x + 3y3x − 2y2?

  • 9x2y: 2 + 1 = 3
  • −4x: 1
  • 3y3x: 3 + 1 = 4
  • −2y2: 2

Polynomial degree = highest = 4.

E. Standard form (descending degree)

Standard form means writing the terms in order of decreasing degree (highest first).

Example

Standard form of −3x8 + 9x2 + 10x? Degrees are 8, 2, 1 — already descending ✓.

Standard form of the polynomial in section D:
3y3x + 9x2y − 2y2 − 4x (degrees 4, 3, 2, 1).

Tip: compute every term's degree first, then write them out in descending order.

F. Classifying by degree name

DegreeNameExample
0Constant7
1Linear6x + 6   or just −2x
2Quadratic5x2 + 9x − 1
3Cubic10x3 − 6x2 − 9x + 12
4Quarticx4 − 3x2 + 1
5Quintic2x5 − x
Combine the two names: "3rd-degree binomial" = degree 3 AND 2 terms (e.g. 4x3 − 7). The degree name and the term-count name are independent — say both.

G. Non-examples — what is NOT a polynomial

A polynomial must have only whole-number exponents on its variables, with no variables in denominators or under roots. Anything else disqualifies it:

ExpressionWhy it's NOT a polynomial
3/x2 or 5x−2variable in denominator / negative exponent
√x or x1/2variable in a root / fractional exponent
2xvariable in the exponent (that's exponential, not polynomial)
1/(x + 4)variable in denominator
Quiz watch-out: if a fraction shows a variable on the bottom (like 33y2/x2), pick "variable in the denominator" or "negative exponent" — they describe the same violation.

H. Practice (10 problems)

Problem 1

Classify 6x − 4x3 + 9x5 − 2 by degree and number of terms.

Show answer
5th-degree polynomial with 4 terms. Highest power is 5 (in 9x5). Four terms.
Problem 2

Which is in standard form?   (a) 5x2 + 2x5 + 7   (b) 2x6 − 4x3 + x   (c) x − 3x4 + 8x2

Show answer
(b). Degrees 6, 3, 1 — fully descending. (a) jumps 2, 5, 0; (c) jumps 1, 4, 2.
Problem 3

Find the degree of x2y − 7xy + xyz + x.

Show answer
3. Term degrees: x2y → 3, −7xy → 2, xyz → 3, x → 1. Highest is 3.
Problem 4

Order by degree (least → greatest):   I. 6x2   II. 18x3 + 5ab − 6y   III. 8a − 5   IV. 4x3y + 3x2 − xy − 4

Show answer
III, I, II, IV. Degrees: I=2, II=3 (the x3), III=1, IV=4 (the x3y → 3+1=4).
Problem 5

Order by number of terms (greatest → least):   I. x + 2xyz   II. 3x + y + z   III. 2x2y + x2 − 3x + 4   IV. 9x2yz

Show answer
III, II, I, IV. Term counts: III=4, II=3, I=2, IV=1.
Problem 6

Which is a 3rd-degree binomial?   (a) 2x3 + x − 5   (b) 4x3 − 7   (c) 5x2 − 9   (d) x3

Show answer
(b). Binomial = 2 terms; degree 3 = highest exponent 3. (a) is a trinomial; (c) is degree 2; (d) is a monomial.
Problem 7

Classify −2x.

Show answer
Linear monomial (degree 1, 1 term).
Problem 8

Why is 33y2/x2 NOT a polynomial?

Show answer
Variable in the denominator (equivalently: x has a negative exponent, x−2). Polynomials cannot have variables in the denominator.
Problem 9

Identify the cubic expression:   (a) 19x4 + 18x3 − 16x2 − 12x + 1   (b) 10x3 − 6x2 − 9x + 12   (c) −9x2 − 3x + 4   (d) 4x + 3

Show answer
(b). Cubic = degree 3. (a) is quartic, (c) quadratic, (d) linear.
Problem 10

Put in standard form: 9x2y − 4x + 3y3x − 2y2.

Show answer
3y3x + 9x2y − 2y2 − 4x. Term degrees: 9x2y → 3, −4x → 1, 3y3x → 4, −2y2 → 2. Descending: 4, 3, 2, 1.

🔑 Key things to remember

  • Multi-variable degree = SUM of exponents in each term. 3y3x is degree 4, not 3.
  • Standard form = descending degree. Compute every term's degree first.
  • Degree name + term-count name are independent. "5th-degree binomial" = degree 5 with 2 terms.
  • Not a polynomial: variable in denominator, variable in a root, fractional or negative exponent, or variable in the exponent (2x).
