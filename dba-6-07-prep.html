6.07 — Polynomial Operations DBA (written)

Mrs. Goldstein's DBA is a typed reflection: 4 questions, write 2–4 thoughtful sentences each. Draft your own first, then check a sample. The reflection questions (2–4) should be specific to you — generic answers lose points.

4 questions · type in Buzz 2–4 sentences each ~15 min
What earns points: specific examples (not "I learned a lot"), honest self-assessment, and one concrete thing you'd change or a tool you'd use. The math question (Q1) wants a real example of each, not just a definition.

The 4 questions

Q1 — 3rd-degree binomial vs. 2nd-degree trinomial Q2 — One challenge in this unit and how you overcame it Q3 — Are you taking good notes? Pacing? Q4 — Do you read the announcements? Tips for typing your answers Pre-submit checklist

Q1 — What's the difference between a 3rd-degree binomial and a 2nd-degree trinomial? Give an example of both.

Math content — the answer is specific

Define each clearly and provide ONE example of each.

Sample answer (2–4 sentences): A third-degree binomial has a degree of 3 (its highest exponent is 3) and exactly 2 terms — for example, 4x3 − 7. A second-degree trinomial has a degree of 2 and exactly 3 terms — for example, 5x2 + 9x − 1. The two differ in both the highest exponent (3 vs. 2) and the number of terms (2 vs. 3).
Other valid examples: a 3rd-degree binomial = x3 − 8 or 2y3 + 5. A 2nd-degree trinomial = x2 − 6x + 9 or 3a2 + 2a − 1.

Q2 — What's one challenge you had in this unit? How did you overcome it?

Reflection — make it specific to you

Name ONE concrete challenge (not "everything was hard") and a real fix that worked. Use the sample as a starting frame.

Sample answer (most common Module 6 struggle): One thing that tripped me up early was subtracting polynomials — I kept forgetting to distribute the negative across every term of the second polynomial, which gave me wrong signs on the middle terms. I overcame it by writing an extra step where I rewrite the subtraction with all the signs already flipped before combining like terms. After doing it a few times the pattern stuck, and on 6.02 I stopped making sign errors.
Pick whichever feels true:
  • Multi-variable degree ("I forgot the degree of a term like 3y3x adds the exponents → degree 4, not 3").
  • Perfect-square middle term ("I kept writing (x − 9)2 = x2 + 81 and missing the −18x").
  • Function composition order ("I had to learn which function goes inside in word problems — the action that happens first").
  • Exponent rules ("I confused power-of-a-power with product rule — multiplied when I should have added").
Then say HOW you fixed it: re-read the lesson, practiced more, made a sticky-note rule, did sim quizzes till it stuck, etc.

Q3 — Notes & pacing self-assessment

Reflection — be honest

Mrs. Goldstein asks four things: Are you taking good notes? Are they organized? Are you rushing/steady/too slow? What helps OR what could you improve? Hit each part.

Sample answer (steady + organized + one honest improvement): I'm working at a steady pace and my notes are organized by sub-topic (6.01, 6.02, etc.) with the rule at the top and a worked example next to it. What helps me most is writing the worked example right next to the rule — that way when I'm taking a quiz I can look at a real problem instead of just a formula. One thing I could improve is reviewing my notes the day before a quiz, instead of only flipping through them while I'm taking it.
Adjust to be honest:
  • If you're rushing: say so and name a fix (e.g., "I'll slow down on the practice test before attempting the real one").
  • If notes are messy: own it and pick one structure to try (separate page per sub-topic, color-code rules, etc.).
  • If you mostly use the lesson pages as notes (or this app): say that and explain how you use them.

Q4 — Do you read the announcements?

Reflection — short, honest

Yes OR no, plus a real reason. Either answer is fine — the teacher wants to see self-awareness.

Sample (yes): Yes, I check the announcements at the top-right of Buzz when I log in so I don't miss reminders about due dates or test windows. Knowing what's coming up helps me plan when to take each quiz and the practice test before the real one.
Sample (no, with a fix): Honestly I usually go straight to my to-do list and the activity I'm working on, so I haven't been reading the announcements consistently. I'll start skimming them at the beginning of each session so I don't miss deadlines or hints for upcoming work.

Tips for typing your answers

  • Be specific. "I struggled with the negative sign in subtraction" beats "I struggled with algebra."
  • Use Module 6 vocabulary in Q1 and Q2 — degree, term, binomial, trinomial, distribute, closure, FOIL. Shows you actually learned it.
  • 2–4 sentences = 30–80 words. Don't write a paragraph; don't write a single sentence.
  • Reflection ≠ flattery. "I learned a lot and Mrs. Goldstein is a great teacher" isn't an answer. Pick something real.
  • One concrete fix or tool per reflection question (Q2, Q3) — give the grader something specific to point to.
  • Match your own tone — if a sentence feels like a robot wrote it, rewrite it in your words.

🔑 Quick recap

  • Q1: degrees differ (3 vs 2) AND term counts differ (2 vs 3). Give one example of each.
  • Q2: name one specific Module 6 challenge + one specific fix.
  • Q3: pace + organization + ONE improvement. Hit all three.
  • Q4: yes/no + real reason. Either is fine.
