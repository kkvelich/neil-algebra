6.07 — Discussion-Based Assessment Prep

The DBA is a short verbal check (a phone or video call with your teacher, roughly 10–15 min). She asks you to explain Module 6 concepts and walk through example problems out loud — no multiple choice, no formulas to memorize, just demonstrating that you understand the material.

The DBA grades how well you can explain — not whether you got every number right. If you blank on arithmetic, that's OK. If you can't describe the method, that's the problem. Practice talking through the steps out loud, in your own words.

6.01 — Classifying polynomials

Likely DBA question

What is a polynomial? Give an example of something that is NOT a polynomial, and explain why.

Talk through this: A polynomial is an expression made of terms added or subtracted, where each term is a number times one or more variables raised to whole-number powers (0, 1, 2, 3, …). For example, 3x2 − 5x + 7 is a polynomial.

Something like 3/x2 is NOT a polynomial because it has a variable in the denominator (equivalently, a negative exponent: x−2). Other non-examples: anything with a variable under a square root (√x), a fractional exponent (x1/2), or a variable in the exponent (2x).
Likely DBA question

How do you classify a polynomial like 3y3x + 9x2y − 2y2 − 4x by degree and number of terms? What's "standard form"?

Talk through this: Count the terms — they're separated by + or −. Four terms here, so it's a polynomial (1 = monomial, 2 = binomial, 3 = trinomial, 4+ = "polynomial"). For degree with multiple variables, I add the exponents in each term: 3y3x → 3+1 = 4; 9x2y → 2+1 = 3; −2y2 → 2; −4x → 1. The polynomial's degree is the highest, which is 4.

Standard form means writing the terms in descending order of degree: 3y3x + 9x2y − 2y2 − 4x (degrees 4, 3, 2, 1).

6.02 — Adding & subtracting + closure

Likely DBA question

Walk me through subtracting (5x2 + 2x − 6) − (3x2 − 6x + 2). What's the most common mistake?

Talk through this: First, distribute the negative to every term in the second polynomial — that's the most common mistake. So I rewrite it as: 5x2 + 2x − 6 − 3x2 + 6x − 2. Then I combine like terms: x2 terms: 5 − 3 = 2 → 2x2. x terms: 2 + 6 = 8 → 8x. Constants: −6 − 2 = −8. So the answer is 2x2 + 8x − 8. People forget to flip the signs on the second and third terms.
Likely DBA question

What is the closure property? Are polynomials closed under all four operations?

Talk through this: Closure means: when you combine two things from a set using an operation, the result stays in that set. Polynomials are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication — adding, subtracting, or multiplying two polynomials always gives another polynomial.

Polynomials are NOT closed under division. Dividing two polynomials can give you a result with a variable in the denominator (like (x+1)/(x−2)), which isn't a polynomial.

6.03 — Exponent rules

Likely DBA question

What are the main exponent rules? Pick one and show me an example.

Talk through this: The big rules are:
  • Product (same base): add exponents. x3 · x5 = x8.
  • Quotient: subtract exponents. x7/x3 = x4.
  • Power of a power: multiply exponents. (x2)5 = x10.
  • Power of a product: distribute the exponent. (3x)4 = 34x4 = 81x4. Don't forget the coefficient!
  • Negative exponent: flip across the bar. x−2 = 1/x2.
  • Zero exponent: anything to the 0 = 1.
Pick one and explain the rule in your own words plus do a quick example.

6.04 — Multiplying polynomials

Likely DBA question

Walk me through multiplying (2x − 3)(x2 + 4x − 5). What method do you use?

Talk through this: I distribute each term of the first polynomial across the trinomial — basically an extended FOIL.

First, 2x times the trinomial: 2x · x2 = 2x3; 2x · 4x = 8x2; 2x · (−5) = −10x.
Then −3 times the trinomial: −3 · x2 = −3x2; −3 · 4x = −12x; −3 · (−5) = 15.
I add everything together and combine like terms: 2x3 + (8 − 3)x2 + (−10 − 12)x + 15 = 2x3 + 5x2 − 22x + 15.
Likely DBA question

What are the two special-product shortcuts?

Talk through this: Two patterns worth memorizing:
  • Difference of squares: (a − b)(a + b) = a2 − b2. The middle terms cancel. Example: (5x − 3)(5x + 3) = 25x2 − 9.
  • Perfect square: (a ± b)2 = a2 ± 2ab + b2. The middle term is the easy one to forget. Example: (3x − 8)2 = 9x2 − 48x + 64.

6.05 — Function composition

Likely DBA question

What does function composition mean? How do you evaluate f(g(3)) if f(x) = 2x + 1 and g(x) = x2?

Talk through this: Composition means feeding the output of one function into another — you do the inside one first, then the outside.

For f(g(3)) with f(x) = 2x + 1 and g(x) = x2: g(3) = 9, then f(9) = 2(9) + 1 = 19.

And order matters — f(g(x)) is usually different from g(f(x)).
Likely DBA question

A bike is 30% off, then you pay 12% tax on the sale price. If c(p) is the sale price and f(c) is the price with tax, which composition gives the final price — c[f(c)] or f[c(p)]? Why?

Talk through this: Discount happens FIRST, so c goes inside (innermost). Then tax is applied to the discounted price, so f goes outside. The composition is f[c(p)]. If c(p) = 0.7p and f(c) = 1.12c, then f[c(p)] = 1.12(0.7p) = 0.784p.
Likely DBA question

If s(t) makes signs per hour and w(h) is hours per week, what are the units of s(w(h))?

Talk through this: I follow the chain: w takes weeks and outputs hours; s takes hours and outputs signs. So s(w(h)) takes weeks in and gives signs out — units are signs per week. The "hours" unit cancels along the chain.

How to talk on a DBA

  • Talk while you work. Don't go silent — explain what you're doing as you do it. Even small narration like "first I'll combine like terms" earns credit.
  • Use the vocabulary. Words like polynomial, degree, standard form, closure, FOIL, composition, slope, intercept — using them shows you understand the concepts, not just the procedures.
  • It's OK to pause and think. "Let me think through that" is fine. A short pause beats a wrong answer.
  • If you blank, restart from a definition. "A polynomial is…" — going back to basics often unblocks you.
  • Arithmetic slips happen. Catch yourself: "Wait — let me redo that step." The teacher is grading explanation, not arithmetic perfection.
  • If you don't know, say so. "I'm not sure" is better than guessing — she'll usually rephrase or move on.
  • Have a calculator and paper handy. Write as you talk; some teachers ask you to show work on camera.

🔑 The five things to be ready to explain

  1. What makes something a polynomial (and what doesn't).
  2. How to distribute the negative when subtracting two polynomials.
  3. At least 2–3 exponent rules with examples.
  4. Two special products: difference of squares and perfect square.
  5. How function composition works, and how to figure out which function goes inside in a word problem.
