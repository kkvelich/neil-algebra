6.04 — Multiplying and Dividing Polynomials

Distribute a monomial, FOIL two binomials, multiply binomial × trinomial, and the two special products everyone forgets: difference of squares and the perfect square.

A. Distribute a monomial across a polynomial

Multiply the monomial by each term of the polynomial. Use the product rule on the variables (add exponents).

Worked example

x2(4x − 3)

  • x2 · 4x = 4x3
  • x2 · (−3) = −3x2
  • Answer: 4x3 − 3x2
Worked example

2x4(3x2 + 4x + 1)

  • 2x4·3x2 = 6x6
  • 2x4·4x = 8x5
  • 2x4·1 = 2x4
  • Answer: 6x6 + 8x5 + 2x4
Watch the sign: when the monomial is negative, distribute the negative too. −5x2(4x − 6x2 − 3) = −20x3 + 30x4 + 15x2 → in standard form: 30x4 − 20x3 + 15x2.

B. FOIL — multiplying two binomials

FOIL = First, Outer, Inner, Last. Multiply each pair and combine like terms.

Worked example

(2x − 3)(x + 4)

  • First: 2x · x = 2x2
  • Outer: 2x · 4 = 8x
  • Inner: −3 · x = −3x
  • Last: −3 · 4 = −12
  • Sum: 2x2 + 8x − 3x − 12 = 2x2 + 5x − 12

C. Difference of squares ⭐

Pattern: (a − b)(a + b) = a2 − b2. The middle terms cancel, so the answer has NO middle term.

Examples

(x + 3)(x − 3) = x2 − 9

(8x − 4)(8x + 4) = 64x2 − 16

(6x − 2)(6x + 2) = 36x2 − 4

(5x − 3)(5x + 3) = 25x2 − 9

Recognize the pattern: same two terms, one with +, one with −. If you spot it, skip FOIL and write the answer in one step.

D. Perfect square ⚠️ (the middle-term trap)

Pattern: (a ± b)2 = a2 ± 2ab + b2. The middle term is 2ab — easy to forget.

The trap: (x − 9)2 ≠ x2 + 81 and ≠ x2 − 81. The correct answer is x2 − 18x + 81. Don't drop the middle term.
Examples

(x − 7)2 = x2 − 2(x)(7) + 72 = x2 − 14x + 49

(3x − 8)2 = (3x)2 − 2(3x)(8) + 82 = 9x2 − 48x + 64

(x + 5)2 = x2 + 10x + 25

(8x − 2)2 = 64x2 − 32x + 4

E. Binomial × trinomial (extended FOIL)

Distribute each term of the binomial across the entire trinomial — that's 6 products total. Combine like terms at the end.

Worked example

(2x − 3)(2x2 + 7x − 1)

  • 2x times the trinomial: 2x·2x2 + 2x·7x + 2x·(−1) = 4x3 + 14x2 − 2x
  • −3 times the trinomial: −3·2x2 + (−3)·7x + (−3)·(−1) = −6x2 − 21x + 3
  • Add: 4x3 + (14 − 6)x2 + (−2 − 21)x + 3 = 4x3 + 8x2 − 23x + 3
Worked example

(5x − 6)(3x2 − 4x − 3)

  • 5x · (3x2 − 4x − 3) = 15x3 − 20x2 − 15x
  • −6 · (3x2 − 4x − 3) = −18x2 + 24x + 18
  • Add: 15x3 + (−20 − 18)x2 + (−15 + 24)x + 18 = 15x3 − 38x2 + 9x + 18

F. Word problems

Area = length × width

Worked example

Rectangle with length 3x and width (2x2 − 5x + 4). Area?

A = 3x(2x2 − 5x + 4) = 6x3 − 15x2 + 12x.

Worked example

Square traffic sign with side (8x − 2). Area?

A = (8x − 2)2 = 64x2 − 32x + 4. (Perfect-square pattern — don't drop the middle term!)

(f · g) — distance = rate × time

Worked example

f(x) = 2x2 + 1 is bike speed (mph). g(x) = x + 1 is time (hours). Find (f · g)(2).

  • f(2) = 2(4) + 1 = 9 mph
  • g(2) = 3 hours
  • (f · g)(2) = 9 · 3 = 27 miles (distance)

(f ÷ g) — rate = distance ÷ time

Worked example

f(x) = 4x + 8 is distance (miles). g(x) = x − 1 is time (hours). Find (f ÷ g)(4).

  • f(4) = 4(4) + 8 = 24 miles
  • g(4) = 3 hours
  • (f ÷ g)(4) = 24 / 3 = 8 mph (rate)

G. Closure under multiplication

Polynomials are closed under multiplication — when you multiply two polynomials, the result is ALWAYS another polynomial.

Example

(4x + 6)(2x2 − 8x) = 8x3 − 20x2 − 48x. The product is a polynomial → closure under multiplication is demonstrated.

On a quiz: when asked "does this demonstrate closure?", the answer is always "yes, the result is a polynomial," not "may or may not be."

H. Practice (10 problems)

Problem 1

Multiply: (x + 3)(x − 3).

Show answer
x2 − 9. Difference of squares. Middle terms cancel.
Problem 2

Expand: (x − 7)2.

Show answer
x2 − 14x + 49. Perfect square. Middle term: 2(x)(7) = 14x; sign is negative because (x−7)2.
Problem 3

Expand: (x + 5)2 and explain how it shows closure.

Show answer
x2 + 10x + 25; IS a polynomial. Perfect square. Result is a polynomial → closure under multiplication holds.
Problem 4

Multiply: x2(4x − 3).

Show answer
4x3 − 3x2. Distribute the monomial.
Problem 5

Multiply: 2x4(3x2 + 4x + 1).

Show answer
6x6 + 8x5 + 2x4. Distribute, then add exponents term by term.
Problem 6

A rectangle has sides (2x − 5) and (x + 4). Find its area in standard form.

Show answer
2x2 + 3x − 20. FOIL: 2x·x + 2x·4 + (−5)·x + (−5)·4 = 2x2 + 8x − 5x − 20.
Problem 7

Multiply: (2x − 3)(2x2 + 7x − 1).

Show answer
4x3 + 8x2 − 23x + 3. Distribute 2x and −3 across the trinomial, then combine.
Problem 8

Multiply (4x + 6)(2x2 − 8x) and explain closure.

Show answer
8x3 − 20x2 − 48x; IS a polynomial. Two polynomials multiplied — the result is a polynomial → closure under multiplication.
Problem 9

f(x) = 2x2 + 1 (rate in mph), g(x) = x + 1 (time in hours). Find (f · g)(2) and interpret.

Show answer
27 miles (distance). f(2) = 9, g(2) = 3, product = 27. Rate × time = distance.
Problem 10

f(x) = 4x + 8 (distance in miles), g(x) = x − 1 (time in hours). Find (f ÷ g)(4) and interpret.

Show answer
8 mph (rate). f(4) = 24, g(4) = 3, 24/3 = 8. Distance ÷ time = rate.

🔑 Key things to remember

  • Difference of squares: (a−b)(a+b) = a2 − b2 (no middle term).
  • Perfect square: (a±b)2 = a2 ± 2ab + b2. Always include the 2ab middle term.
  • Binomial × trinomial: distribute each term of the binomial across the whole trinomial — 6 products → combine like terms.
  • Closure under multiplication: product of polynomials is always a polynomial.
  • Word problems: rate × time = distance (f·g). Distance ÷ time = rate (f÷g). Track units.
  • Standard form for the final answer: highest degree first.
