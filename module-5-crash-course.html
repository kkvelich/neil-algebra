A. Representing data (5.01)

Three main displays for one-variable data:

  • Dot plot: one dot per value, stacked on a number line.
  • Histogram: bars over INTERVALS; height = frequency in that bin.
  • Box plot: visual of the five-number summary (min, Q1, median, Q3, max). Box is Q1→Q3 with a line at the median; whiskers reach to min/max (outliers shown as separate dots).
Building a histogram from raw data: count each value (frequency table) FIRST, then match the bar heights to the frequencies. Don't eyeball.

Median, Q1, Q3 from a small data set

Sort first. With n values:

  • Odd n → median = middle value.
  • Even n → median = average of the two middle values.
  • Q1 = median of the lower half. Q3 = median of the upper half.
Example

Data: 6, 9, 12, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24. n=8 → median = (12+15)/2 = 13.5.

Lower half [6,9,12,12] → Q1 = (9+12)/2 = 10.5. Upper half [15,18,21,24] → Q3 = (18+21)/2 = 19.5.

IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 19.5 − 10.5 = 9.

B. Comparing data sets (5.02)

MeasureWhat it tells youRobust to outliers?
MeanAverage (typical) valueNo
MedianMiddle valueYes
Rangemax − min (spread)No
IQRQ3 − Q1 (middle 50%)Yes
Std dev (σ)Average distance from meanNo
Mean vs median rule: symmetric, no outliers → MEAN. Skewed or has outliers → MEDIAN. The median resists distortion.

Comparing two sets: compare centers (mean/median) and spreads (IQR/σ) separately.

C. Outliers (5.04)

1.5·IQR rule: a value is an outlier if it falls below Q1 − 1.5·IQR or above Q3 + 1.5·IQR.

Example

Q1=10, Q3=30, IQR=20. Lower fence = 10 − 30 = −20. Upper fence = 30 + 30 = 60. Any value < −20 or > 60 is an outlier.

Effect of removing an outlier:
  • Removing a low outlier → mean increases.
  • Removing a high outlier → mean decreases.
  • Median barely shifts in either case.

The denominator trap: "Of dog owners, % who own cats" → divide by DOG ROW total. "Of cat owners, % who own dogs" → divide by CAT COLUMN total. Same cell, different denominators → different answers.

E. Scatter plots & line of best fit (5.06)

Scatter plot = dots for (x, y) pairs. Look for: shape (linear / curved), direction (positive / negative), strength (tight / loose).

Slope & y-intercept in context

For y = mx + b:

  • Slope m = rate of change (e.g., "cost rises $6 per item").
  • y-intercept b = value at x = 0 (starting/initial value).

Building a line from two points

Slope = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁). Then use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) → solve for y = mx + b form.

Residuals

Residual = actual − predicted. Small residuals = good fit.

"Good fit" criterion: residuals should scatter RANDOMLY around zero and their SUM should be ≈ 0. A pattern or one-sided residuals = poor fit. "Every residual must be small" is NOT the criterion (some can be larger as long as they balance).

F. Correlation vs causation (5.07)

Correlation coefficient r ranges from −1 to +1.

r valueWhat it means
+1Perfect positive linear
~+0.7 to +0.9Strong positive
~0No linear relationship
~−0.7 to −0.9Strong negative
−1Perfect negative linear
Correlation ≠ causation. Strong r means the variables move together, but a third confounding variable may explain both. Classic example: ice cream sales and drowning incidents both rise — because both are driven by hot weather, not by each other.

⭐ Average rate of change (recurring weak spot)

For values at two x-points:

average rate of change = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

Example

Profit at hour 2 = $30; at hour 6 = $98. Rate of change = (98 − 30)/(6 − 2) = 68/4 = $17 per hour.

⚠ $68 is the TOTAL change, not the rate. Always divide by Δx, AND include units.

🔑 The Module 5 cheat sheet

  • IQR = Q3 − Q1 (the BOX width). Range = max − min. Don't mix them up.
  • Median for skewed/outliers; mean for symmetric.
  • 1.5·IQR rule: outlier if < Q1 − 1.5·IQR or > Q3 + 1.5·IQR.
  • Two-way: pick the right denominator. Row vs column conditional is the trap.
  • Slope = rate of change in context, intercept = starting value.
  • Good fit: residuals random, sum ≈ 0. NOT "every residual small."
  • Correlation ≠ causation — watch for confounding variables.
  • Average rate of change = Δy/Δx WITH UNITS. Don't forget to divide.
